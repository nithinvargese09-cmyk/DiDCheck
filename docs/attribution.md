# Attribution and provenance

The methodological source is:

> Alaparti, Aditya; Katta, Amann; Vargese, Nithin; and Sainani, Swaraj. 2026. *Distributional Pre-Trends: A Variance Diagnostic for Difference-in-Differences Designs*. Working paper (preprint), Zenodo, published October 13, 2026, with technical supplement. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23328869.

The manuscript states: **“Equal Contribution: All four authors contributed equally to this work.”** Its shared affiliation is Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology, Alexandria, Virginia, United States. The author order and equal-contribution statement are preserved here.

The title, four authors, author order, affiliation, and DOI were verified against [Zenodo record 23328869](https://zenodo.org/records/23328869). The paper DOI identifies the research preprint, not the DiDCheck software release. The software, manuscript, technical supplement, and separate replication archive are distinct objects and should be cited accordingly.

DiDCheck packages the manuscript's documented mean and demeaned-variance diagnostics behind a public data-validation and results interface. Software verification of this interface is separate from the manuscript's mathematical arguments and simulation evidence. Neither software tests nor attribution establishes independent empirical validation.

The manuscript's framing acknowledges related distributional DiD and pretrend methods, including Roth and Sant'Anna's functional-form work, changes-in-changes, distribution regression, panel quantile methods, and classical and clustered variance tests. DiDCheck does not claim that prior methods never examined distributional pretrends, or that this diagnostic is an omnibus distributional test.

The China Shock and minimum-wage settings are possible future applications. Neither is a validated application of this diagnostic, and this repository's examples contain synthetic data only.
